Compute a linear layer y = x × WT whose weight matrix W (N × K) has been pruned to 2:4 semi-structured sparsity: along each row, every group of 4 consecutive weights contains at most 2 nonzeros. W is stored in compressed form: w_values (N × K/2) holds the 2 kept values of each group, and w_meta (N × K/4, one byte per group) records where they sit inside the group. The activations x (M × K) are dense. x, w_values and y are 32-bit floats; w_meta is 8-bit unsigned.

For row n and group g (covering columns 4g to 4g+3), let b = w_meta[n, g], i0 = b & 3 (bits 1:0) and i1 = (b >> 2) & 3 (bits 3:2). Then W[n, 4g + i0] = w_values[n, 2g], W[n, 4g + i1] = w_values[n, 2g + 1], and the other two weights of the group are zero: \[ y_{m,n} = \sum_{g=0}^{K/4-1} \Big( \text{w_values}_{n,2g}\; x_{m,\,4g+i_0} + \text{w_values}_{n,2g+1}\; x_{m,\,4g+i_1} \Big) \]

This is the format used by sparse LLM inference (2:4-pruned weights, e.g. SparseGPT/Wanda): in a batched decode step M is small, so the layer is limited by how fast the weights can be streamed from memory, and the compressed weights are only 56% of the dense size.

Implementation Requirements

  • Implement the solve function; do not change its signature.
  • External libraries are not permitted.
  • Write the result into y (M × N, row-major). y is not zero-initialized; every element must be written.
  • Use float32 operands and accumulate in float32 or better; rounding x or the weights to FP16 or BF16 is outside the tolerance.

Example

Input: M = 2, N = 3, K = 8 \[ x = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 & 6 & 7 & 8 \\ 1 & 0 & -1 & 2 & 0 & 1 & -2 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \] \[ \text{w_values} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 2 & -1 & 0.5 & 3 \\ -1 & 1 & 1 & -2 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \text{w_meta} = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 14 \\ 12 & 9 \\ 13 & 8 \end{bmatrix} \] The metadata decodes to position pairs \((0,1),(2,3)\) for row 0, \((0,3),(1,2)\) for row 1 and \((1,3),(0,2)\) for row 2, so the dense weight matrix is \[ W = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 2 & 0 & 0 & -1 & 0 & 0.5 & 3 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 & 0 & 1 & 1 & 0 & -2 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \] Output: \[ y = x \times W^T = \begin{bmatrix} 18 & 22 & -7 \\ 0 & -5.5 & 6 \end{bmatrix} \]

Constraints

  • 1 ≤ M ≤ 256
  • 1 ≤ N ≤ 32,768
  • 4 ≤ K ≤ 8,192, and K is divisible by 4
  • Every w_meta byte satisfies i0 < i1 and has bits 7:4 equal to zero
  • Values of x and w_values are in the range [-1, 1]
  • All tensors are stored in row-major order
  • Performance is measured with M = 16, N = 28,672, K = 4,096
