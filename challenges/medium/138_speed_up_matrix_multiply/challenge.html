Multiply row-major matrices A (M × K) and B (K × N) and store the product in C (M × N). All values are 32-bit floating point numbers.

Your task: make it faster. The starter code is a deliberately slow but correct CUDA kernel. Find the bottleneck, fix it, and reach at least a 1.3× speedup over the starter kernel on the performance test (measured on the same GPU). Your solution must still pass every correctness test.

Implementation Requirements

  • External libraries are not permitted
  • The solve function signature must remain unchanged
  • The result must be stored in C
  • The target is 1.3× the runtime of the provided CUDA starter kernel on the performance test

Example:

Input (M = 2, N = 3, K = 2): \[ A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix}, \quad B = \begin{bmatrix} 5 & 6 & 7 \\ 8 & 9 & 10 \end{bmatrix} \] Output: \[ C = \begin{bmatrix} 21 & 24 & 27 \\ 47 & 54 & 61 \end{bmatrix} \]

Constraints

  • 1 ≤ M, N, K ≤ 4,096
  • Input values are in the range [-1, 1]
  • Target speedup: 1.3× over the starter kernel
  • Performance is measured with M = 4,096, N = 4,096, K = 4,096
